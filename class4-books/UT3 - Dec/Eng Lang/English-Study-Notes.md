# CHAPTER 9: AGREEMENT OF VERB WITH SUBJECT

## Chapter Summary
This chapter explains the fundamental rule that a finite verb must always agree with its subject in both **number** (singular/plural) and **person** (first, second, third). Understanding verb agreement is crucial for writing grammatically correct sentences.

---

## Concept Explanation

### What is a Sentence?
A sentence is a group of words that contains:
- A **finite verb** (main action)
- A **subject** (who/what is doing the action)
- **Complete meaning** (independent, doesn't depend on other words)

### Types of Verbs

#### 1. **Finite Verbs**
- Have tense (past, present, future)
- Directly linked to a subject
- Controlled by the subject
- **Examples:** sleeps, were, saved, went, is, are

#### 2. **Infinitives** (Non-finite verbs)
NOT directly linked to a subject; NOT controlled by subject; NO tense

**a) To-infinitive:** He wanted **to write** a letter
**b) Bare infinitive:** He helped me **finish** the task

---

### Key Rule: Verb-Subject Agreement

A finite verb must agree with its subject in:

#### **(i) NUMBER**
- **Singular subject** → **Singular verb**
  - The dog sleeps on the sofa
  - She plays football
  
- **Plural subject** → **Plural verb**
  - The dogs sleep on the sofa
  - They play football

#### **(ii) PERSON**
**Three persons:**

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|----------|--------|
| **1st Person** | I | We |
| **2nd Person** | You | You |
| **3rd Person** | He, She, It | They |

---

### Examples of Verb-Subject Agreement

| Person & Number | Subject | Verb Form | Complete Sentence |
|-----------------|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1st Sg. | I | am singing | I am singing. |
| 1st Pl. | We | are singing | We are singing. |
| 2nd Sg./Pl. | You | sing | You sing. |
| 3rd Sg. | He/She/It | sings | He sings. |
| 3rd Sg. (Proper Noun) | Govinda | sings | Govinda sings. |
| 3rd Pl. | They/Some birds | sing | They sing. |

---

### Special Case: The Verb "BE"

**"I" behaves unusually with the verb "be":**
- I am a student (singular verb: am)
- I am going home (singular verb: am)
- I was going home (singular verb: was)

**BUT with other verbs, "I" takes plural form:**
- I go home (plural: go, NOT goes)
- I have written a letter (plural: have, NOT has)

---

### Special Rules

#### **Future Perfect Tense**
Uses plural form "have" even with singular subjects:
- He will have sung
- She will have arrived

#### **Collective Nouns**

**Singular verb** - collective noun seen as ONE unit:
- The English team **was** defeated (team = single unit)

**Plural verb** - collective noun seen as individual members:
- The crew **were** English and Australian (crew = individual people)

   **CORRECT:** Ronnie likes reading mystery stories, I like travelogues.
   *(Reason: Ronnie = 3rd person singular (likes); I = 1st person (like))*

4. **INCORRECT:** My friends has decided to go to Varanasi this winter.
   **CORRECT:** My friends have decided to go to Varanasi this winter.
   *(Reason: "friends" is plural)*

5. **INCORRECT:** My brother and the tall boy called Mihir is playing drums.
   **CORRECT:** My brother and the tall boy called Mihir are playing drums.
   *(Reason: Two subjects joined by "and" = plural)*

6. **INCORRECT:** Though this dish look tempting, you must not eats it.
   **CORRECT:** Though this dish looks tempting, you must not eat it.
   *(Reason: "dish" = singular (looks); "you" takes "eat" not "eats")*

7. **INCORRECT:** Mathematics are a difficult subject.
   **CORRECT:** Mathematics is a difficult subject.
   *(Reason: Mathematics is singular despite -s ending)*

8. **INCORRECT:** The people is celebrating their country's victory in the World Cup.
   **CORRECT:** The people are celebrating their country's victory in the World Cup.
   *(Reason: "people" is plural)*

9. **INCORRECT:** The children was laughing at the antics of the clown.
   **CORRECT:** The children were laughing at the antics of the clown.
   *(Reason: "children" is plural)*

10. **INCORRECT:** Where has all my friends gone? When am they coming back?
    **CORRECT:** Where have all my friends gone? When are they coming back?
    *(Reason: "friends" = plural (have/are); "they" = plural (are not am)*

11. **INCORRECT:** There is, in that room, twenty men ready to die for their country.
    **CORRECT:** There are, in that room, twenty men ready to die for their country.
    *(Reason: "men" is plural)*

12. **INCORRECT:** Does you not know what 'a score' mean?
    **CORRECT:** Do you not know what 'a score' means?
    *(Reason: "you" takes "do" (not does); "score" = singular (means)*

13. **INCORRECT:** No boy can runs faster than John.
    **CORRECT:** No boy can run faster than John.
    *(Reason: "can" is modal; use bare infinitive "run")*

14. **INCORRECT:** He does not ever tells the truth.
    **CORRECT:** He does not ever tell the truth.
    *(Reason: With "does," use bare infinitive "tell")*

15. **INCORRECT:** He could sees a light in the distance.
    **CORRECT:** He could see a light in the distance.
    *(Reason: With "could" (modal), use bare infinitive "see")*

# CHAPTER 21: USE OF CAPITAL LETTERS

## Chapter Summary
Capital letters (uppercase letters) follow specific rules in English writing. They are used to mark the beginning of sentences, proper nouns, titles, acronyms, and other important elements.

---

## Rules for Using Capital Letters

### Rule 1: BEGINNING OF SENTENCES
Every sentence begins with a capital letter.
- **Correct:** She walked to school every morning.
- **Incorrect:** she walked to school every morning.

### Rule 2: PROPER NOUNS
Always capitalize proper nouns (names of specific people, places, things):

**a) Names of people:**
- John, Mary, Rajesh, Priya

**b) Nationalities and languages:**
- Indian, English, French, Marathi, Hindi

**c) Days and months:**
- Monday, September, December
- (Note: Seasons are NOT capitalized: spring, winter, summer, autumn)

**d) Names of places:**
- India, Mumbai, Taj Mahal, Himalayas, Thames River

**e) Names of organizations/institutions:**
- United Nations, Indian Railways, St. Xavier's School

**f) Names of specific events:**
- World War II, Independence Day, Diwali

**g) Titles of books, movies, songs:**
- First word and main words capitalized
- "Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone"
- "The Sound of Music"

### Rule 3: THE PRONOUN "I"
Always capitalize the pronoun "I" (even in the middle of sentences).
- I went to the market.
- She and I are friends.
- My brother told me that I should study harder.

### Rule 4: TITLES AND HONORIFICS
Capitalize titles before names:
- Dr. Sharma, Mr. Patel, Professor Singh, Queen Elizabeth

### Rule 5: IMPORTANT WORDS IN TITLES
In titles of books, movies, articles, capitalize the first and last words and all important words (NOT articles, prepositions, or conjunctions unless they're the first word):
- "The Lord of the Rings"
- "A Tale of Two Cities"
- "Pride and Prejudice"

### Rule 6: ACRONYMS AND ABBREVIATIONS
- NASA, FBI, UNICEF, BBC, WHO
- Dr. (Doctor), Mr. (Mister), Mrs. (Mistress), etc.

### Rule 7: DIRECT QUOTATIONS
Capitalize the first word of a direct quotation:
- He said, "I will come tomorrow."
- She asked, "Where are you going?"

### Rule 8: RELIGIOUS REFERENCES
- God, Allah, Buddha (deity names)
- Christian, Hindu, Muslim (religion names)
- The Bible, The Quran, The Torah

### Rule 9: DAYS/MONTHS vs. SEASONS
- ✓ CAPITAL: Monday, June, October
- ✗ LOWERCASE: summer, winter, spring, autumn

---

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

**WRONG:** she is a teacher
**CORRECT:** She is a teacher

**WRONG:** I live in new delhi
**CORRECT:** I live in New Delhi

**WRONG:** me and john are friends
**CORRECT:** John and I are friends (Note: Also uses "I" rule)

**WRONG:** The book "the catcher in the rye" is famous
**CORRECT:** The book "The Catcher in the Rye" is famous

# CHAPTER 22: PROVERBS

## Chapter Summary
A proverb is a well-known phrase or sentence that gives advice or expresses something generally true. Proverbs are born from common human experience and contain practical wisdom.

---

## What is a Proverb?

**Definition:** A brief, memorable saying that expresses a general truth or offers advice.

**Other names:** Saying, adage, maxim

**Characteristics:**
1. **Brief and precise** - uses few words
2. **Memorable** - easy to remember
3. **General truth** - applies to many situations
4. **Contains wisdom** - born from experience
5. **Often gives advice or warning**

---

## 12 Major Proverbs with Meanings

### 1. **Better late than never.**
- **Meaning:** If you must do something, do it even if late rather than not doing it at all
- **Lesson:** Fulfilling obligations matters more than timing

### 2. **Call a spade a spade.**
- **Meaning:** Be honest and frank; say exactly what you think
- **Lesson:** Speak truthfully and avoid insincerity

### 3. **Do as you would be done by.**
- **Meaning:** Treat others as you wish to be treated; golden rule
- **Lesson:** Practice empathy and kindness

### 4. **Don't put the cart before the horse.**
- **Meaning:** Do things in the correct order
- **Lesson:** Maintain logical sequence; prioritize correctly

### 5. **Empty vessels make the most sound.**
- **Meaning:** Foolish, unintelligent people speak the most
- **Lesson:** Wisdom often comes in silence

### 6. **Fortune favours the brave.**
- **Meaning:** Courageous people who take risks are most often successful
- **Lesson:** Courage and risk-taking lead to success

### 7. **Never say die.**
- **Meaning:** Never give up; keep trying
- **Lesson:** Perseverance is key to success

### 8. **Practice makes perfect.**
- **Meaning:** Repeated practice makes you skilled
- **Lesson:** Excellence requires dedication and repetition

### 9. **Rome was not built in a day.**
- **Meaning:** Great achievements take time and effort
- **Lesson:** Be patient and persistent in achieving goals

### 10. **Waste not, want not.**
- **Meaning:** Use resources wisely; don't waste unnecessarily
- **Lesson:** Conservation prevents scarcity

### 11. **Make hay while the sun shines.**
- **Meaning:** Act immediately when opportunity comes; don't delay
- **Lesson:** Seize opportunities quickly
- **Related proverbs:**
  - Strike while the iron is hot
  - Never put off till tomorrow what you can do today
  - Gather ye rosebuds while ye may

### 12. **Too many cooks spoil the broth.**
- **Meaning:** When too many people manage one task, confusion results
- **Lesson:** Excessive oversight or management creates problems
- **Similar proverb:** Two captains will sink a ship

---

# CHAPTER 28: WRITING A STORY

## Chapter Summary
Story writing transforms given outlines into complete narratives. The key is developing points into detailed, engaging prose with dialogue, proper tense, and an appropriate title.

---

## Key Rules for Story Writing

### Rule 1: UNDERSTAND THE OUTLINE
- Read the outline carefully
- Understand the main plot and message
- Develop points into full sentences
- Add your own interesting details

### Rule 2: MAKE THE STORY ENGAGING
- **Animate characters:** Make animals talk and behave like humans
- **Add dialogue:** Include conversations between characters
- **Use descriptions:** Paint vivid pictures for readers
- **Create emotion:** Make readers care about the story

### Rule 3: USE PAST TENSE
- **Why:** Stories narrate events that already happened
- **Correct:** "The boy walked to school..." (NOT "walks")
- This creates a narrative distance and formal story tone

### Rule 4: WRITE AN APPROPRIATE TITLE
- Should relate to:
  - Important characters, OR
  - Key events, OR
  - Story's moral lesson
- **Format:** Use one of these (from Chapter 21):
  - Capitalize first and last words + main words
  - Do NOT use quotation marks
  - Do NOT use punctuation
- **Examples:**
  - "The Wind and the Sun" ✓
  - "The Crows and the Black Snake" ✓
  - "The Farmer and the Goose" ✓

---

## EXAMPLE A: Wind and the Sun (Short Version)

### Outline:
Wind says stronger than Sun → Sun disagrees → see man → Wind tries to remove coat → fails → Sun tries → succeeds → Wind admits defeat

### STORY: The Wind and the Sun

One day, the Wind boasted that he was much stronger than the Sun. The Sun beamed and said that he was much stronger and he could prove it. The Wind was quite annoyed.

"There is a man walking down the road. Just watch me. I shall make him take off his coat," said the Wind. He huffed and he puffed with all his might. The man only drew his coat tighter round himself to keep warm.

"Let me try," said the Sun when the Wind was tired of blowing. He shone down fiercely on the man. His rays were hot and piercing. The man began to feel hot. He took off his coat and started wiping the sweat from his brow.

"I have won," smiled the Sun.

"Yes, you have," admitted the Wind.

**Moral:** Gentleness is often more effective than force.

---

## EXAMPLE B: The Crows and the Black Snake (Detailed Version)

### Outline:
Crows live in tree → snake eats babies → crows sad → plan to leave → seek Fox's help → Fox's clever plan with jewels → servants follow → snake killed → crows grateful

### STORY: The Crows and the Black Snake

Mr and Mrs Crow lived happily in an old, banyan tree, with their family of little baby crows. But then, an evil black snake came to live in a hole at the bottom of the tree trunk.

One morning, Mr Crow and his wife flew out of their nest to bring food for their young ones. As soon as the snake saw them leave, he slithered up the trunk and ate all the little, fluffy nestlings. Mr and Mrs Crow were very sad and cried a lot but could not do anything as they were frightened of the snake.

"I am afraid we have to leave this tree," said Mr Crow sadly.

"But we have lived here for such a long time. I love this place," said his wife.

"Let's go to our friend Mr Fox and ask him what to do. He is a clever fellow and will certainly think of a way to help us," said Mr Crow.

So they went to Mr Fox and told him the whole sad story. Mr Fox thought for a while and then said, "Why should you leave your home? You have lived happily there for so long. Listen, tomorrow the ladies of the palace will come down to the river to have a bath. They will keep their clothes and jewels on the bank. You must pick up some of the jewels and fly away. The servants, guarding the jewels, will certainly see you and follow you to get the jewels back."

"And then?" cried Mrs Crow.

"Then you must fly back to the banyan tree and drop the jewels into the black snake's hole."

Mr and Mrs Crow did what Mr Fox told them. The palace servants followed them, shouting loudly. They then put their sticks into the hole to recover the jewels. The snake came hissing out, ready to attack. The servants surrounded the snake and beat it to death. Then they took the jewels out of the hole and went away.

Mr and Mrs Crow were delighted. They thanked Mr Fox over and over again for the wonderful plan that had helped them to get rid of the dreadful black snake.

**Moral:** Cleverness and planning can overcome physical danger.

## HOW TO WRITE YOUR STORY: Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Read and Understand
- Read outline multiple times
- Identify main characters
- Note key plot points
- Understand the moral

### Step 2: Plan Your Story
- Introduction: Set the scene
- Middle: Develop conflict
- Climax: Peak of action
- Resolution: How it ends
- Moral: What we learn

### Step 3: Write in Past Tense
- "The dog was sleeping..." (NOT "is sleeping")
- "She walked to school..." (NOT "walks")
- "They found the treasure..." (NOT "find")

### Step 4: Add Life to Your Story
- **Dialogue:** What characters say (use quotation marks)
- **Descriptions:** How things look and feel
- **Emotions:** How characters feel
- **Details:** Make it vivid and real

### Step 5: Choose an Excellent Title
- Related to main character or event
- Engaging and memorable
- Proper capitalization
- No quotation marks or punctuation

### Step 6: Review and Edit
- Check tense consistency
- Verify spelling
- Ensure story follows outline
- Confirm clear moral

---

## COMMON MISTAKES IN STORY WRITING

**WRONG (Present Tense):** The farmer finds a goose and brings it home. It lays eggs every day.

**CORRECT (Past Tense):** The farmer found a goose and brought it home. It laid eggs every day.

---

**WRONG (No Dialogue):** The wife wanted the farmer to cut the goose. The farmer disagreed.

**CORRECT (With Dialogue):** "Let's cut the goose and get all its gold," said the wife.
"But it lays eggs for us every day," replied the farmer.

---

**WRONG (Missing Title):** A story about mice and a cat...

**CORRECT (Clear Title):** The Bell for the Cat
